## Priority decision

I prioritise evidence integrity, accountable decisions and recovery before breadth. This responds to the [official role's](https://semble.bamboohr.com/careers/212) emphasis on an operating model with measurable impact. It does not assume Semble has any gap in its current tooling.

I use an impact/effort matrix to expose assumptions rather than present invented ROI. Impact is ordinal: **high** materially changes the safety or clarity of an operating decision, **medium** improves recurring workflow quality, **low** improves convenience. Effort is provisional: **small** can be tested in the present bounded workflow, **medium** needs user research or several components, **large** needs integration/identity/security work. These are planning judgements, not measured delivery estimates. Dependencies and confidence can override a simple ranking.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Sensitive rejection, duplicates and ambiguity | Operators can distinguish usable evidence from volume or uncertainty. | High / small | Necessary integrity controls in this synthetic case. | Build and evaluate now. |

The prototype’s opportunity score is a transparent evidence-volume aid: 20 points per independent supporting fixture provenance, capped at 100. It is not customer value, confidence, ROI or an automatic priority. My impact/effort memo and a human rationale remain separate from that count.

## Why I did not start with broad integrations

The [automation page](https://www.semble.io/platform/healthcare-automation) already advertises extensive connectors, error handling and audit trails. A new integration is not automatically a valuable operating product. I would first ask whether existing tools plus a better cadence solve the problem. Broad ingestion also introduces retention, authority and sensitive-data risks before the workflow has earned adoption.

## Outcome-tied 12–18 month proposal

This is a conditional planning horizon from a future pilot start, not a funded commitment or an asserted delivery schedule. At every checkpoint I would choose **keep**, **rebuild** or **retire** against observed outcomes and current alternatives.

| Horizon | Work and decision | Outcome evidence required | Keep / rebuild / retire rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Months 0–3: discover and establish baseline | Observe current handoffs; test synthetic workflows; instrument matched task quality/time and owner clarity. | Baseline with denominators/windows; operator objections; actual failure scenarios; privacy/security feasibility. | Keep if users recognise a material problem and controls are feasible. Rebuild definitions or flow if misunderstandings dominate. Retire if existing tooling solves it better. |
| Months 3–6: narrow internal pilot | Pilot one approved nonclinical workflow with training, manual fallback and release rehearsal. | Correct decision rate, active task time, owner completeness, false blocks, assistance and retention. | Keep if decision quality improves and effort is acceptable. Rebuild if quality improves but adoption or correction burden is poor. Retire if no useful outcome emerges. |
| Months 6–9: harden only what works | Add verified identity, role separation, tenant/security controls, retention/deletion and approved monitoring where needed. | Independent security/privacy review; restore/rollback rehearsal; operator outcome improvement sustained. | Keep only with controls and ongoing user value. Rebuild risky or fragile components. Retire components whose cost exceeds use. |
| Months 9–12: extend cautiously | Add a second team or one justified trusted source; evaluate a production extractor only if approved. | Comparable cohort outcomes, subgroup quality, source provenance and stable recovery; measured support cost. | Keep expansion if quality and accountability hold. Rebuild schema/taxonomy for real ambiguity. Retire redundant automations. |
| Months 12–18: consolidate the operating model | Connect validated insights to quarterly planning; review every workflow and dashboard. | Outcome trend with denominators, decision use, ongoing adoption and total ownership effort. | Keep workflows changing useful decisions. Rebuild where objectives changed. Retire unused dashboards, duplicative tools and ineffective processes. |

## Capacity and stop conditions

I would assign one accountable pilot owner and protect capacity for baseline collection, onboarding and exception handling. Implementation effort alone is an incomplete cost model: reviewers, training, corrections, support and governance also consume time.

Stop or restrict the pilot if sensitive data is entered, authority is unclear, a recovery path cannot be rehearsed, a control fails, participants cannot understand gate failures, or the workflow's measured decision quality is worse than the current process. A passing synthetic beta policy cannot override those conditions.

I would revisit the priority memo monthly. Changes must cite new user/evaluation evidence, the displaced work and the revised outcome hypothesis; a polished dashboard alone is not evidence of impact.
